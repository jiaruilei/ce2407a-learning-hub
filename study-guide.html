Which method should I use?

Start with the engineering question. Open the closest match, write down the event or parameter, then check the assumptions before calculating.

Finding a probability

Will one specimen, or the average of several specimens, cross a limit?

One specimen. Use the distribution of the individual measurement. For a normal strength with mean μ and SD σ, a limit L has standard score (L − μ)/σ.

A sample average. For n independent specimens with the same distribution, the mean has standard error σ/√n. If the population is normal, the average is normal for every n. For example, if σ = 8 MPa, use 8 MPa for one core and 2 MPa for the average of 16.

Does a warning tell me how likely a defect is?

Condition on the observation. Write P(defect | warning). Make two branches: defective pieces that trigger a warning, and sound pieces that trigger one. The first branch divided by the sum of both is the answer.

Check the denominator. If only 1% of pieces are defective, a sensor can catch most defects while still producing many false warnings. You need the prevalence and both warning rates.

Am I counting events or waiting for the first one?

Independent trials with constant p. Fix the number of trials and count successes with the binomial model. Count the trials up to and including the first success with the geometric model.

Continuous time or distance. For a homogeneous Poisson process with rate ν, the count in exposure t is Poisson with mean νt. The time to the first event is exponential. As a cross-check, waiting beyond t and observing zero events by t have the same probability, exp(−νt).

Do two failures or two uncertain loads act together?

System events. If either failure stops the system, use a union. If both must fail, use an intersection. Write P(A ∩ B) = P(A)P(B | A). Substituting P(B) for the conditional probability requires independence.

Load and resistance. Write the safety margin G = R − S. Its variance is Var(R) + Var(S) − 2Cov(R,S). A shared environmental driver can make the covariance material. To use a normal tail for failure, check that the margin is normal, as it is for jointly normal inputs.

Drawing conclusions from data

Do I need a value for a model parameter, or evidence that the model fits?

Estimate a parameter. Choose a distribution from the engineering process. For a moment estimate, equate a theoretical moment to its sample counterpart. An exponential wait with sample mean 4 hours gives a fitted rate of 1/4 per hour.

Assess fit. Convert the model probabilities into expected bin counts and compare them with observed counts using a chi-square goodness-of-fit test. Pool sparse bins where needed. With k final bins and m parameters fitted from the sample, use k − 1 − m degrees of freedom.

Does the requirement concern the typical value or the consistency of production?

Target mean. A confidence interval estimates μ. A test compares it with a specified μ₀. For independent normal observations, use z with known population SD, or t with n − 1 degrees of freedom when the sample SD replaces it.

Target spread. For independent observations from a normal population, use the chi-square variance statistic (n − 1)s²/σ₀². If the concern is excessive variability, use the upper tail. Square the standard deviations before substituting.

Am I describing one quantity or predicting its response to another?

One quantity. A histogram shows the distribution of a measurement. For unequal bin widths, use density heights so that the area represents the sample fraction. Record units and inspect the tails around any engineering threshold.

Paired quantities. For load and its measured deflection, plot each pair together. If a straight line is plausible, regress deflection on load. The slope has units of deflection per unit load. Look for curvature in the residuals and check whether the prediction lies inside the measured load range.
